### The allowlist entry, and the boundary inside it

`.gl-` is **not** the glossary alone. `.lb-pagescroll:has(.lb-cockpit) > .gl-wrap` belongs to
`GlobalLeaderboard.jsx` (`<div className="gl-wrap mt-5">`) and shares two letters with this screen and
nothing else. This is `.up-banner` again, and MENU-38 again: **a boundary drawn around the most
visible carrier eventually catches something that merely has the name.** The entry is therefore
`/\.gl-(?!wrap)/`, and — because that exclusion is invisible to every other check, the rule carrying no
literal at all — it has a counter-check of its own, written in H-e's shape: *`.gl-wrap` must still
exist in the tree, and no rule coming in through M6's prefixes may carry it.* Widen the expression to
`/\.gl-/` and it goes red; nothing else in the suite notices.

### The fifth silent seam

`glossary-desktop.test.js` documented four ways the desktop glossary breaks without throwing. The
migration adds a fifth: **the head's fill now arrives through the variable the inline style reads.**
`STICKY_HEAD_BG` emits `var(--sf-head)`; the rule re-points `--sf-head` on the element; the `!important`
is gone. Two silent breaks follow — the constant going back to a literal (the inline value then wins
and the head paints the modal fill), and the re-point being deleted (the head paints `--sf-head`).

The guard **computes** rather than compares spellings, the way `fx-panel.test.js` does at `.cz-stage`:
the rule's own custom properties are substituted into its `background` and the result must be the
resolved `--sf-head-fade`, character for character. **With a negative probe** — it must *not* be the
resolved `--sf-head` — because without it the check would pass on a tree where the re-point was gone
and both sides read the same step. A too-wide expression calms a ratchet exactly as reliably as a
too-narrow one.
