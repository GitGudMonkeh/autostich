else:

1. the cell column sits **flush** with the letters, top and bottom, instead of breaking the cap line;

## Part 3 — the measurement

### 3.1 How tall is an Orbitron capital

The design says *"rund `.7em`"* and builds the overshoot on it. Flush turns that rounded figure into a
load-bearing one, so it was measured on the real font — and once at the rendered size was not enough:

| font size | ink above the baseline | ratio |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 88 px | 64 px | 0.727273 |
| 176 px | 127 px | 0.721591 |
| 352 px | 253 px | **0.71875** |
| 880 px | 632.5 px | **0.71875** |
| 1760 px | 1265 px | **0.71875** |
| 3520 px | 2530 px | **0.71875** |

**`actualBoundingBoxAscent` is quantised to whole pixels**, so at the size the screen actually renders
the answer has a resolution of 1/88 and reads 0.7273. From 352 px upward it converges on **0.71875**,
which is 23/32 — a clean font metric, not a measurement artefact.

**And every capital gives the same number** — `A U T O S H I R C K`, round shapes included. Orbitron
draws its bowls flat-sided, so there is **no overshoot**, and "flush" has exactly one meaning here.
That is not true of most typefaces and is the reason this section exists (C5-F01).

### 3.2 The route the owner offered does not exist, and that is a finding rather than a substitution

> *"notfalls die Abstände zwischen den Quadraten dementsprechend anpassen"*

Taken literally — keep the `.09em` square, take the difference out of the gaps:

| cell | resulting gap | at 88 px | at 1280 (after `zoom: 0.85`) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **`.09em`** | **−0.00018em** | **−0.02 px** | **negative — the squares would overlap** |
| `.085em` | 0.00554em | 0.49 px | 0.41 device px |
| `.08em` | 0.01125em | 0.99 px | 0.84 device px |
| `.075em` | 0.01696em | 1.49 px | 1.27 device px |

**Eight cells at `.09em` measure `.72em` and the cap is `.71875em`** — the column is already taller
than the letters before a single gap is counted. Even the rows that are possible put the gap under one
device pixel at the binding viewport, where the squares would merge into a bar.

### 3.3 So the column scales, by one factor, and the proportion is untouched

**The letter is an *excerpt* of the sign** — that is the design's central claim — so its cell-to-gutter
proportion may not change. A different rhythm would make it a second drawing. The whole column is
therefore scaled until eight cells and seven gaps fill the cap exactly:

```
factor = .71875 / .874 = .822368
```

| | design | scaled | at 88 px | at 1280, device px |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| cell | `.09em` | `.074013em` | 6.51 | 5.54 |
| gutter | `.022em` | `.018092em` | 1.59 | 1.35 |
| border | `.012em` | `.009868em` | 0.87 | 0.74 |
| radius | `.014em` | `.011513em` | 1.01 | 0.86 |
| **check** | | **8 × cell + 7 × gutter = .718750em** | | |

**None of those four numbers is written anywhere.** The viewBox already carries the design's table, so
setting the **height** scales everything inside it and `width: auto` takes the width from the aspect
ratio. One value in the stylesheet, and the four above are its consequences.

### 3.4 Is it flush? Two numbers, not a look

Measured against the **ink** of the capitals, not the line box — a line box is taller than the letters
and would call almost anything flush. The baseline is located by planting a zero-height inline-block
inside the wordmark; the cap top is the baseline minus the measured ratio.

| | 1280 × 720 de | 1920 × 1080 de | 1280 × 720 en | 1920 × 1080 en |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| bottom edge vs the baseline | **0.00 px** | **0.00 px** | **0.00 px** | **0.00 px** |
| top edge vs the cap line | **0.12 px** | **0.12 px** | **0.12 px** | **0.12 px** |

**The bottom is exact by construction**: `vertical-align: baseline` puts a replaced inline-block's
bottom margin edge on the baseline, so there is no correction value to drift. The 0.12 px at the top is
the sub-pixel placement of that box, an eighth of a pixel.

**The first run of this measurement reported 0.75 px and was wrong** (C5-F04). It took the cap ratio at
88 px, where Chrome's whole-pixel rounding turns a 63.25 px cap into 64 — so the instrument reported a
mismatch the layout did not have. The probe now takes the ratio at 3520 px and scales it. **An
instrument that rounds where the thing it measures does not will report a defect that is its own.**

### 3.5 The fill

The unlit cells of the column take the **mid tone of the design's own three-state table** — deck colour
at 28 % fill and 66 % edge — instead of the quiet state's 3 % white.

**No value is introduced; a different one from the same table is chosen.** And it is that table's own
rule applied to its own case: it says the three states are calibrated for the **dark screen ground**
and may be pulled back on a lighter one. The column's ground is not a screen, it is a row of solid
88 px letterforms, so the step goes **up**.

**Scoped to the column.** The standalone mark beneath the tagline stands on the screen ground the table
was calibrated for and keeps the quiet state. **The two cuts now differ, and that is visible rather
than buried:** `evidence/C5/owner/hub-*.png` shows both in one frame, and the ladder either side of the
shipped step is in `evidence/C5/owner/fill-*-{a-before,b-half,c-ships-mid,d-stronger,e-full}.png`, at
true 1× and at both sizes.

## Part 4 — findings

| ID | Finding | Disposition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **C5-F01** | **Orbitron has no overshoot.** Every capital measured — round ones included — gives the same ink height, `.71875em`. In most typefaces `O` and `S` exceed `H` by 1–2 %, and "flush" would then have to pick a reference | Recorded, because it is why this change is exact rather than approximate. It also means the number can be trusted at any size: the ratio converges from 352 px upward |
| **C5-F02** | **The route the owner offered is arithmetically impossible.** Keeping the `.09em` square and taking the difference out of the gaps needs a gap of **−0.02 px**: eight cells at `.09em` are `.72em` and the cap is `.71875em` | Reported rather than silently substituted. The column scales as a whole instead — which also keeps the cell-to-gutter proportion identical to the sign's, so the letter stays an *excerpt* rather than becoming a second drawing |
| **C5-F03** | **The design's line calling the overshoot deliberate is superseded**, and two of its numbers with it: the cap is `.71875em` and not "rund .7em", and the column is `.71875em` and not `.874em` | Struck through in `docs/mainscreen-marke.md` with the decision, the date and the reasoning beside it, in the same shape Q1a used. The measure table above it still holds **as a proportion** |
| **C5-F04** | **The first flush measurement reported a 0.75 px mismatch that did not exist.** It read the cap ratio at the rendered size, where Chrome quantises `actualBoundingBoxAscent` to whole pixels — a 63.25 px cap comes back as 64 | Fixed by taking the ratio at 3520 px. Kept in the file with the note: an instrument that rounds where its subject does not will report a defect that is its own |
| **C5-F05** | **The two cuts of the sign now carry different unlit states** — the column at the mid tone, the standalone mark at the quiet one | Deliberate and scoped: the mark stands on the ground the table was calibrated for. **Shown rather than argued** — both appear in one frame in the owner set, so extending the change to the mark is one look away |
| **C5-F06** | **The wordmark is another 2 px narrower** (632 → 630), because the column is 1.4 px narrower than the one C2 shipped | Recorded. The collision point moves to 97.8 px; Q6 unaffected |
