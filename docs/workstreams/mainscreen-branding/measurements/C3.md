## Part 2 — the zero-delta gates

### 2.1 Below 1280 px — zero

Six cells, three widths, both languages; the wordmark's box and every resolved type property, the
document's scroll height, and the box of every child of `.hub-root`:

| | result |
| --- | --- |
| cells with any delta, C2 → C3 | **0 of 6** |

**Structural, not careful.** Everything C3 touches is inside `.as-deck`, which is `hidden dt:flex` and
therefore has no box below the threshold, or inside the `@media (min-width: 1280px)` block.
`deck-tafel.test.js` asserts the second half as *"no `.as-deck*` rule outside the desktop section"*
rather than trusting it.

### 2.2 Every surface but the hub — and one viewport where more moved, on purpose

**The five in-run surfaces are at zero on the raw comparison.** `skill-choice`, **`run-stage`**,
`perk-choice`, `architect` and `victory` appear nowhere in the report, at any viewport, in either
language. The run replaces the hub, so those captures contain none of it — **the wordmark trap did not
fire**, measured rather than argued.

**Outside the hub, four of five viewports are at zero.** C2's filtered comparison, widened here from
`.hub-play` to `.hub-root` — because C3 changes `.hub-stand`, which is `.hub-play`'s sibling:

```
node evidence/C2/outside-the-hub.mjs --prefix 0/0/2/0   --before evidence/C2/after --after evidence/C3/after
```

| viewport | deltas outside `.hub-root` |
| --- | --- |
| 1280 × 720 | **0** |
| 1536 × 791 | **0** |
| 1600 × 900 | **0** |
| 1920 × 1080 | **0** |
| **1400 × 700** | **2624** |

**And the 2624 are the fix, not a regression.** Two measurements say so and neither is an argument:

1. **Every one of them is a box coordinate.** `box[x]` 1029 · `box[w]` 979 · `box[y]` 441 · `box[h]`
   175 — and **nothing else at all**: no background, no border, no radius, no shadow, no colour. A
   restyle leaking out of the hub would show up as paint. This is pure reflow.
2. **The page got 8 px wider, at that size and nowhere else.** The usable width of the document:

   | | 1280 × 720 | 1400 × 700 | 1536 × 791 | 1600 × 900 | 1920 × 1080 |
   | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
   | cells whose width changed | 0 of 32 | **22 of 32** | 0 of 32 | 0 of 32 | 0 of 32 |
   | before → after | — | **1392 → 1400** | — | — | — |

   1400 × 700 carried a **vertical scrollbar** because the page overflowed by 14 px, and the scrollbar
   took 8 px of width from every screen behind it. C3 removes the overflow (§3.3), the scrollbar goes,
   and eleven menu surfaces get their 8 px back. **The ten in-run cells at that size did not change** —
   they scroll for their own reason and did before.

> So the honest statement is not *"every surface but `hub` at zero"* — it is: **every surface but the
> hub is at zero wherever the page geometry did not change, and at the one viewport where it did, the
> change is 8 px of width that this commit gave back.**

## Part 3 — the formula, and the two ways it was wrong

### 3.1 What shipped

```css
.hub-pair, .hub-foot { --hub-zoom: clamp(0.85, tan(atan2(100vw, 1920px)), 1); zoom: var(--hub-zoom); }
.as-deck-art { height: clamp(112px, calc((100vh - 80px) / var(--hub-zoom, 0.85) - 592px), 268px); }
```

**Two terms, because the page has two kinds of height.** `U` = 80 px is the vertical chrome **outside**
the zoom, in CSS px: `.app-root` pads 1.5 rem top and bottom, `.hub-root` adds 6 px of head padding,
an 18 px gap and 4 px of foot padding — 76 px by the rules, **79.4 measured**. `L` = 592 px is
everything **inside** the zoom that is not the art — the rest of the panel, the tile bank and the foot
band — **591.3 measured**.

**Both are solved from two measurements, not guessed.** `pageHeight(art = 0) = U + zoom · L` is linear
in the zoom, so two zoom values determine both; the remaining six viewports check the solution.

| viewport | zoom | predicted max art | measured max art |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1280 × 720 | 0.850 | 162.3 | **162.3** |
| 1400 × 700 | 0.850 | 138.8 | **138.7** |
| 1536 × 791 | 0.850 | 245.9 | **245.8** |
| 1700 × 760 | 0.885 | 177.4 | **177.2** |
| 1800 × 820 | 0.938 | 198.7 | **198.4** |
| 1920 × 850 | 1.000 | 179.3 | **179.3** |

**0.3 px across the range.** The rule enters 80 and 592 rather than 79.4 and 591.3 — both roundings go
**up**, so the art lands about 1.3 px under the maximum. A formula that goes to the decimal overflows
on the first rounded pixel.

### 3.2 The two ways it was wrong, and neither was visible at the contract's viewports

> **All five viewports the contract names sit exactly on the zoom clamp's floor.** The zoom only rises
> above 0.85 from 1632 px of width. So a formula whose divisor is the zoom can be wrong across the
> entire upper band and look right at every size anyone was asked to check.

**First version — one term, divided by the floor.** `(100vh - 583px) / 0.85`. Zero overflow at all
five contract viewports. Measured at three sizes in the band the contract does not name:

| | 1700 × 760 | 1800 × 820 | 1920 × 850 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| overflow, first version | **28 px** | **66 px** | **89 px** |

**Second version — divided by the real zoom, still one term.** `(100vh - 583px) / var(--hub-zoom)`.
Better and still wrong: **20 · 51 · 88 px**. The divisor was not the whole error — a single constant
cannot stand for both a zoomed and an unzoomed term, and the fit only looked right because every
sample had the same zoom.

**Third version — two terms.** Zero at all eight, both languages.

**How the band came to be measured at all:** `headzone.mjs` was extended from the contract's five
viewports to eight, with the three chosen for what they exercise rather than for coverage — the zoom
between its floor and 1. That extension is the whole difference between this commit being right and
being right-looking (C3-F01).

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1280 × 720 | 0.850 | 112 × 154 | **117 × 161** | 370 | 0 → **0** |
| 1400 × 700 | 0.850 | 112 × 154 | **100 × 137** | 347 | **14** → **0** |
| 1536 × 791 | 0.850 | 112 × 154 | **177 × 244** | 454 | 0 → **0** |
| 1600 × 900 | 0.850 | 112 × 154 | **194 × 268** | 477 | 0 → **0** |
| 1920 × 1080 | 1.000 | 112 × 154 | **194 × 268** | 477 | 0 → **0** |
| 1700 × 760 | 0.885 | 112 × 154 | **128 × 176** | — | 0 → **0** |

**The design size is reached at 1600 × 900 and above**, which is what option A promised. **1400 × 700
stops scrolling** — the 14 px it was carrying before this workstream began were the deck art's, and
the art is what pays them back (C1-F05, and the owner was told this when choosing).

**The remaining slack is 0.5 to 1.8 px** at the six constrained viewports: the art is filling the
column to within a pixel and a half, which is what "as large as the page carries" has to mean if it
means anything.

### 3.4 The design's 196 is the card's own aspect, rounded

Measured: the deck card renders **194 × 268** at the cap. The design asks for 196 × 268 — **the same
card, rounded up by two pixels.** So the rule sets the **height only** and lets the width follow the
image; forcing 196 would stretch the artwork by 1 %, and the artwork is a named non-goal (C3-F03).

### 3.5 Three deviations from the design, each with its number

| # | The design says | What shipped, and why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **D1** | the attribute rows are **stacked under** the framed field | **They stay beside it.** Built stacked and measured first: it costs **39 px at all three small viewports**, and the right column has **7** at 1280 × 720 (C1-F03). Beside the art it costs nothing — the panel is as tall as the taller of art and name column. The contract's own wording is *"the **existing** battlefield and FX lines **restyled** as the attribute chip row"* |
| **D2** | a **196 px** wide field | **194 px** — see §3.4. Two pixels, and they are the ones that would distort the card |
| **D3** | deck **tier**: three pips and "Stufe II von III" | **Not built.** The data is `resolvePackByDeckId(activeDeckId).tiers`, computed in `App.jsx` and not passed to `StartScreen`. Adding a prop means editing `App.jsx`, which is a **named non-goal**. Same shape as Build DNA's exclusion: the panel does not grow a data path this task may not open |

**"Framed, with corners"** is read as the frame the art already carries — a 1 px edge and a radius.
Corner ornaments would be a new decoration, and a new icon or glyph is a house-rule question for the
owner, not a worker's choice.

### 3.6 Two of the four inline alphas, converted rather than copied

The contract: *"an inline literal is unreachable by any rule that is not `!important` — so if C2 or C4
touches one of those lines, **convert it to a `var()` rather than copying it**, and say so."*

| Alpha | Where it was | Where it is now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `.25` | inline on the deck art's `border` | `.as-deck-art`, one rule |
| `.22` | inline on the music box's `border` | `.as-deck-attr`, one rule — and the chip row would have **copied it three times** |
| `.10` | inline on the bonus lane | **untouched** — C3 does not go there |
| `.18` | inline on `.as-hub-stripe`'s `borderLeftColor` | **untouched** |

**The value did not change; its reachability did.** A `var()` is not available — minting a token is
forbidden and the ladder's edge steps are opaque — so the conversion is from *inline literal* to
*stylesheet rule*, which is precisely what the clause protects: a rule can be reached by the cascade
and by `panel-tokens.test.js`, and an inline literal cannot. C4 takes it the rest of the way or
records it in the ratchet.

`deck-tafel.test.js` asserts both halves: the literal is **gone from the JSX** and present in **exactly
one rule**. A check on the rule alone would be green while the literal sat inline three lines away.
