# C1 — the head-zone budget, re-derived

**Commit** `C1` · Tier C · branch `task/mainscreen-brand` · base `2600c74f` (tip of
`feature/desktop-menus`) · worktree `C:/Code/Autostich-worktrees/menu-rework` · ports preview 5189 ·
survey 5181.

**Measurement only. No pixel moves.** No file under `src/`, `index.html` or `test/` is touched by this
commit; Part 2 proves it rather than asserting it.

**Why this is a commit and not a bullet.** C1's numbers are the input to a decision the owner has to
make, and a worker that has already composed the head zone has an interest in the answer. The
contract says so; this record is what that sentence buys.

**What it was asked to answer, in one line:** *does the head zone hold wordmark, tagline and the
5 × 8 mark at 1280×720?* **It does, at the mark's full size, with the page at zero overflow — and the
same measurement moves the workstream's top risk from C2 to C3.**

**The baseline re-confirms the noise floor across a session boundary, and that was not planned for**
(C1-F15). `src/` and `index.html` are byte-identical between the tip of `feature/desktop-menus` and
the `f65785dc` merge that M11's own after-capture measured — so the two matrices should agree if the
harness is deterministic across workers, machines and days, and nobody had ever checked that:

```
node scripts/surface-delta.mjs   docs/workstreams/desktop-menus/evidence/M11/after   docs/workstreams/mainscreen-branding/evidence/C1/baseline

  compared 160 cells, 25189 matched nodes
  unmatched nodes: 0 pre-registered (H-c: leaderboard, victory), 0 elsewhere
  ZERO computed deltas on the four surface axes.                       exit 0
```

**The two files are not byte-identical** — the matrix carries per-run bookkeeping, the run counts
among it (M11-F10) — and that is exactly why the comparator is the instrument and `sha256sum` is not.

**Why a second instrument and not a survey cell.** The survey answers *did anything move*. C1 asks
*how much room is there*, and that number does not exist in the rendered tree — only in what the tree
would tolerate. So the budget is measured by **adding height until the document scrolls and reporting
the last value that did not.** A bisection, not a sum.

That choice is the direct answer to the failure the contract names: four design documents went to
workers this round, all four held on observation and failed on prediction, and one had its height
arithmetic stale in all three terms. **A sum of row heights and gaps is a prediction. The largest
spacer the layout accepts is an observation**, and it stays right even where I have misunderstood
which rule is firing.

## Part 3 — the budget, re-derived

*Everything §1.3 of the planning report says about this budget was measured before the typography
round landed, and that round moved 41.9 % of menu text by more than 5 %. Its figures are not restated
as fact anywhere below. These replace them.*

### 3.1 What the viewport gives, and what fires

Measured at 1280×720 **first**, before any other size.

| | 1280×720 | 1400×700 | 1536×791 | 1600×900 | 1920×1080 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `zoom` on `.hub-pair`, declared | 0.85 | 0.85 | 0.85 | 0.85 | 1 |
| `(min-width:1280) and (max-height:950)` | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | — |
| `(min-width:1280) and (max-height:900)` | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | — | — |
| `(min-width:1280) and (max-height:820)` | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | — | — |
| `.hub-root` gap / padding-top | 18 / 6 | 18 / 6 | 18 / 6 | 18 / 6 | 40 / 24 |
| `.hub-play` row gap | 14 | 14 | 14 | 14 | 22 |
| `.hub-pair` column gap | 56 | 56 | 56 | 56 | 80 |
| document overflow **as it stands today** | 0 | **14 px** | 0 | 0 | 0 |

**All three short-desktop blocks match at 1280×720, and only one of them carries a hub rule**
(C1-F06). `max-height: 900` carries `.rn-shell` and `.rn-bank` — the run stage. `max-height: 820`
carries nothing at all: its contents were removed by `#viewport-1280` and the block is now a comment.
So the hub's short-desktop rhythm is **one block, not three**, and "all three fire" is true of the
media queries and false of the hub.

**The effective zoom was read off the element rather than off the rule** — `offsetHeight` against
`getBoundingClientRect().height` — so the number holds whatever the clamp resolved to and whether or
not the engine understood `tan(atan2(…))`. It agreed with the declared 0.85 to within the integer
rounding of `offsetHeight` (0.8495 on the pair, 0.8587 on the foot, the latter a 40-px-tall box where
one rounded pixel is 2 %). **The agreement is the point; the two decimals are the instrument, not a
finding.**

### 3.2 The two columns, and where the constraint actually is

*All lengths below are in the units a **child of `.hub-play`** writes — inside the `zoom`, which is
what a composition has to spend. Multiply by 0.85 for CSS px on screen at the four smaller sizes.*

| | 1280×720 | 1400×700 | 1536×791 | 1600×900 | 1920×1080 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `.hub-play` height today | 438 | 438 | 438 | 438 | 468 |
| `.hub-stand` height today | 705 | 705 | 705 | 705 | 712 |
| **free height in the left column** *(before it becomes the taller of the two)* | **267** | 267 | 267 | 267 | 244 |
| **budget for one new row** *(bisected; +1 row gap on top)* | **259.5** | 0 | 343 | 471.5 | 427.5 |
| **growth the right column tolerates** *(bisected on the panel itself)* | **7** | **0** | 90.5 | 219 | 206 |

**`.hub-pair { align-items: center }` is the fact the budget turns on.** The pair is as tall as its
**taller** column, and that is the right one at every viewport. So the first 267 px the left column
grows come out of the difference to the right column and cost the page nothing; only after that do
they cost height. The right column has no such cushion: **every pixel it grows is a pixel of page.**

**§1.3's premise is wrong in sign, and this is the finding that matters most** (C1-F02). It says the
tagline and the mark are *"two new rows in a column that has already spent its reserves at the size
that matters most"* — marked *inferred*, from the rules. Measured, at that exact size: the left column
has **267 px of free height before it costs anything at all**, and the two new rows cost 128.

### 3.6 The deck panel, and the decision C1 hands to the owner

*This is not in C1's brief as written. It is here because C1's own numbers put it there: the head
zone is safe and the panel is not, and it is cheaper to price this now than to discover it in C3 with
the panel already composed.*

The design grows the deck art from today's **112 × 154** to a **196 × 268** framed field. Tried on
the live page, at every viewport, in both languages:

| | 1280×720 | 1400×700 | 1536×791 | 1600×900 | 1920×1080 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `.hub-stand` 705 → | 819 | 819 | 819 | 819 | 826 |
| **page overflow** | **91 px** | **111 px** | **20 px** | 0 | 0 |

> **The 114 px is a floor, not the figure.** The trial resized the art **where it stands** — beside
> the name column, in a `flex items-center` row — so the row grew to `max(268, text)` and the panel
> grew by the difference. The design **stacks** them: framed field, then the name and level beneath
> it. Stacked, the panel grows by the art's full height minus what the row already spent, which is
> more. **So every overflow in the table above is the smallest it can be**, and the option pricing
> below inherits that. Naming it here rather than re-measuring it in C1: the stacked panel does not
> exist yet, and measuring a composition nobody has built is the prediction this record refuses to
> make elsewhere.

**Three ways out, each measured rather than named.** All three were applied to the live page, the
page asked whether it scrolls, and everything put back (`restored: true` in all ten cells).

| | **A — the art fits the viewport** | **B — the tile bank gives way** | **C — the zoom floor drops** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| what it does | the art is as tall as the page allows and reaches 196 × 268 only where that is true | the art is 196 × 268 everywhere; the four-row tile bank below is capped until the page fits | the art is 196 × 268 everywhere; `.hub-pair`'s `zoom` clamp floor goes below 0.85 |
| **1280×720** | art **161 px** — 7 px more than today, 107 short of the design | bank **328 → 221**, gives up **107 px** of four rows at 82 px each | zoom **0.85 → 0.738** |
| **1400×700** | art **138 px** — **16 px *less* than today** | bank **328 → 198**, gives up **130 px** | zoom **0.85 → 0.713** |
| **1536×791** | art **245 px** | bank gives up **23 px** | zoom **0.825** |
| **1600×900 · 1920×1080** | art **268 px** — the design size, reached | nothing given up | unchanged |
| what it costs | the deck panel is the *subject* of the screen at two viewports and roughly today's size at the other three | a panel this design does not touch loses a third of its height at the size that matters most | see C1-F14 — the cost cannot be bounded against the rule that sets the floor |

**A fourth is not priced and is named instead: let the page scroll at 1280×720.** Scrolling the
itch.io embed is the one outcome the 1280 threshold was moved to prevent (`index.css`, *WARUM 1280*),
so pricing it would have suggested it is on the table.

**C1 does not choose.** The numbers are above; the decision is the owner's, and it blocks **C3 only** —
C2 proceeds against §3.5 either way.

### 3.7 Centring the head zone, tried rather than argued

The design wants the head zone **centred** and argues it costs nothing because every block below
runs the full column width. That is a prediction about four boxes. Applied to the live page —
`align-items: center` on `.hub-play`, every child's left edge and width recorded before and after:

| | 1280×720 | 1400×700 | 1536×791 | 1600×900 | 1920×1080 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| children that moved | **1 of 5** | 1 of 5 | 1 of 5 | 1 of 5 | 1 of 5 |
| which one | the wordmark | the wordmark | the wordmark | the wordmark | the wordmark |
| by how much | +16.34 px | +30.92 px | +30.92 px | +30.92 px | +30.55 px |
| the other four | **x and width unchanged** | unchanged | unchanged | unchanged | unchanged |

**The design's prediction holds** (C1-F13). The bonus lane, the tutorial offer, the play group and
the seed/leaderboard row all run the full column width already, so centring reaches the wordmark and
nothing else. This is the one design prediction in this document that was re-measured and came back
exactly as written.
